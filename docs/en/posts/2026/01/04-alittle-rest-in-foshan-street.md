# A little Rest in FoShan Street

**By Polya** · July 30th

     Finally,I have arrived QuanFu Study to which I keep stared at the watch all the way.Few minutes earlier than yesterday,but the queque a few more longer.It's can't be worse that there's no seat when I finally got in.However,I had no choice but to leave.


     I  was going to company with Deyuan who volunteered at the study.And now I have to make another choice.I took on B70 and planed to go to the FoShan Study where I had had good time.I eventually stood at the stop of Ginza Mall that I have been familiar with.Ginza Tower stands under the blue sky,with golden cover shining the flash.。 I moved towards the street hidden in the shade——the FoShan Street。And all but sought for a moment again。The storefronts along the street are staggered in a zigzag line, and the sign for "Spring City Study" squeezes in among them so unobtrusively you could easily miss it. Small as it is, the place opens up into a hidden world once you step inside. It used to be packed every time I came—this is the first time I've been able to leave my bag on a seat, with plenty of empties around me. The study is tastefully decorated, and the atmosphere carries a soothing, healing quality that wraps you in calm. Even if you do nothing at all, it would be just right to curl up and take a good nap here. The warm-toned lamps cast a cozy glow, and outside the light slowly begins to fade, as if to set the mood. Mist crept across the windowpanes, and then—right on cue—the rain started to fall, perfectly timed. The rains lately have been capricious like that.
     
     
     Remembering why I came, I fixed my gaze on the bookshelves and scanned. Fortunately, the books are finely categorized. I looked around for the letter Q, zeroed in on the section, and began browsing carefully. I had a feeling I'd find it—and sure enough, I did. Triumphant, I pulled out The * We Are The Electric *and settled back into my seat, thoroughly absorbed in reading.
     

But before long, my stomach started growling. My eyes drifted aimlessly over the pages, while my mind ran calculations on how far it was to Grandma Shao's place nearby. And after that, I couldn't stop thinking about her! "It's dinner rush hour now—it'll be packed. Better wait a bit," I tried to talk myself into staying. But my body had already disobeyed and got up from the seat. Before I knew it, I was practically sprinting out of the study.


     Outside, the rain had stopped at some point without my noticing. The last rays of the fierce sun were beginning to seep back through the shade of the trees. Braving the heat, I pressed on without a pause. At the end of the street, there it was—the familiar little eatery. I walked in and ordered without hesitation: a bowl of duck blood vermicelli soup and a scallion pancake—15 yuan all together. When they appeared before me after such a long absence, I couldn't tell whether the mist on my glasses was from the steam or from my tears. It was still scalding hot—my greatest trial. I forced myself to hold back, went and got a cup of barley tea, and took small sips while eyeing the temptation in front of me. I waited until the temperature finally subdued the food enough for it to obediently enter my mouth, and then I launched into battle. I let my taste buds bathe in the savory broth, occasionally taking bites of the pancake dipped in chili sauce—absolutely divine. After savoring every last bit, I patted my round belly and left, fully content.


     Back at the study, I worked on my English homework for a while, then decided it was time to go. Sunlight filtered through the clouds and layers of foliage, finally landing on the street in dappled patches, uneven and scattered. As I walked along, the light shifted between brightness and shadow, as if I were passing through different weathers, different moments. The street scenery kept changing under the alternating play of sun and shade. Light and shadow took turns illuminating the old corners of the lane, as if quietly telling their stories. Stepping out of the street felt like turning the last page of a book—and a pang of emotion followed.
